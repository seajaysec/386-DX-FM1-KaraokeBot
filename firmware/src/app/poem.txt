# SLAMJAM's words (tools/gen_poem.py packs them into build/gen/poem_data.h). Earnest performances about silly,
# ordinary things, madlib style: a poem picks a frame (how it is told: an ode, a protest, a eulogy, a sportscast ...),
# a topic and a hero, then fills a stanza plan (poem.h) from the frame's templates. The topic's words fill the slots,
# but now and then a wildcard from anywhere takes a slot instead, so a poem about trucks can meet a rubber chicken.
# Family-friendly by construction: only the words here can be said. ASCII only; a line expands to 63 characters at
# most when its slots take their likely words (gen_poem.py checks; a line that runs over is made again);
# tools/check_poem.py says every word with the FM-1's own voice, under the sanitizers.
#
# Slots, the topic's: {H} the hero, {HS} heroes; {n} a noun, {ns} nouns; {a} an adjective; {p} a place; {v} a verb.
# Wildcards, from anywhere: {x} a thing, {xs} things; {X} a strange adjective; {l} a strange place; {m} a silly verb;
# {k} someone; {f} the poem's core (one set for the whole poem: food, tools, gadgets ...); {e} an exclamation; {s} a sound; {N} a number; {t} a time; {d} a way of doing it;
# {g} a grand noun; {G} a grand adjective. {T} (the intro only) is the poem's title.
# A noun written "goose/geese" has its plural after the slash; with no slash the plural adds s.
#
# @frame NAME starts a frame (NAME is what it is called: A NEW ODE). Then its kinds: @intro, @title, @open, @image,
# @ask, @rise, @vow, @refrain, @return, @close. A kind a frame leaves out comes from the first frame (slam).
# @topic NAME, then H: n: a: p: v: lines, comma separated. @wild, then the wildcards' lines. @core NAME, then its
# words: each poem picks one core, and every {f} in it comes from there.
